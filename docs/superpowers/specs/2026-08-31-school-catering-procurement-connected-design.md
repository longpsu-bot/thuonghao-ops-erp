# School-Catering Procurement Connected Design

**Status:** Written design for user review  
**Date:** 2026-08-31  
**Architecture authority:** OPS_SYSTEM_MAP v1.0 / ARCH-002  
**Repository baseline:** `main` at `9928ced99e9d0430801140ad29f86b716d594e59`  
**Scope:** Connected school-catering Planning → Procurement flow, supplier allocation families, PO drafts, PO release, and operator workbench.  
**Out of scope:** released-PO amendment/cancellation/replacement, supplier acknowledgement, Warehouse receiving, Dispatch execution, Finance, Retool mutation, Live OPS mutation, and Admin numbering remediation.

## 1. Executive decision

The first connected school-catering Procurement slice will preserve the proven operational strengths of OPS v1 while enforcing Atlas domain ownership, versioning, transaction safety, and immutable supplier commitments.

The visible operator flow is intentionally simple:

```text
PLANNING
Confirmed Need
  → Chuyển sang lên đơn
  → Purchase Handoff released

PROCUREMENT
Phân bổ nhà cung ứng
  → supplier-family allocation and rebalancing
  → Tạo đơn mua

Đơn mua
  → PO drafts by supplier + service date
  → Phát hành cho NCC
  → official PO number generated by backend
  → RELEASED_TO_SUPPLIER
  → PDF / export
```

Purchase Handoff remains a durable backend boundary but is not a separate operator screen. Procurement begins with the first real purchasing decision: supplier allocation.

The Allocation Family is the Procurement working aggregate. It gives operators the v1 ability to split and rebalance quantities while Atlas preserves exact Purchase Handoff lineage underneath it.

## 2. Contract precedence and non-conflict with existing Atlas work

This design does **not** replace PA-05E or redesign the existing supplier-direct wholesale slice.

### 2.1 PA-05E remains authoritative for supplier-direct wholesale

The existing bounded wholesale contract remains valid for its own scope:

```text
Released Dispatch Requirement
→ exact one-supplier-per-line fulfilment allocation
→ released Supplier Purchase Order
```

Its public commands, tables, tests, privileges, and one-supplier-per-requirement-line simplification must remain behaviorally compatible.

### 2.2 This design governs school catering

School catering has different proven operating requirements:

- many schools and delivery locations;
- the same ingredient may be supplied by more than one supplier;
- operators work against an aggregated school/date/ingredient demand family;
- split quantities must balance exactly to the family total;
- saved split ratios are useful for deterministic rebalance after demand changes;
- one supplier/date PO may contain lines for many schools.

These requirements are evidenced by OPS v1 and require a separate school-catering Procurement contract rather than forcing school catering through PA-05E's wholesale simplification.

### 2.3 UI and review implication

The dormant `src/modules/procurement/ProcurementWorkbench.tsx` prototype is not an implementation baseline. It is fixture-driven and exposes a broader seven-step prototype lifecycle. The connected school-catering UI must be built from the contract in this document and authoritative Atlas reads/commands.

The current `pa-06a-operator-workflow-matrix.md` CMD-05/CMD-06 supplier-direct flow must not be interpreted as a school-catering one-supplier-per-line requirement. It remains evidence for the wholesale slice unless explicitly amended by a later shared contract.

## 3. OPS v1 operational evidence to preserve

Retool is workflow evidence only, not Atlas architecture authority.

The useful Purchase Planner behaviors to preserve are:

- date-range and multi-school working scope;
- dense master/detail tables rather than dashboard-heavy screens;
- exception-focused filters such as unassigned and quantity mismatch;
- a family grain equivalent to service date + school + ingredient;
- family-atomic supplier allocation saves;
- multiple supplier split rows per family;
- positive `split_qty` values;
- exact validation that supplier splits sum to the family total;
- persisted `split_ratio` values;
- rebalance behavior that preserves ratios and allocates rounding residual deterministically;
- PO grouping by supplier + service date;
- PO lines retaining school and ingredient detail;
- supplier-oriented PDF/XLSX output.

Atlas must **not** copy these v1 characteristics:

- direct SQL from UI;
- broad page coupling between Procurement, Warehouse and Dispatch;
- PO lines that are silently deleted and rebuilt after the PO has become a supplier commitment;
- user-entered system codes or official document numbers;
- fixture/local state as authority.

## 4. Authoritative domain flow

### 4.1 Planning boundary

The existing school-catering Confirmed Need command:

`release_confirmed_needs_for_purchase_handoff`

moves the batch to `RELEASED_FOR_PURCHASE_HANDOFF`, but it does not materialize a school-catering Purchase Handoff.

The visible Planning action `Chuyển sang lên đơn` therefore executes two durable backend steps:

1. release Confirmed Need for Purchase Handoff;
2. materialize and release the school-catering Purchase Handoff.

If step 1 succeeds and step 2 fails, the UI shows the valid intermediate state and a retry action. The application must not pretend both operations rolled back.

### 4.2 Procurement boundary

After Purchase Handoff release, Planning quantities are immutable input facts for Procurement.

Procurement may decide:

- supplier assignment;
- quantity split between eligible suppliers;
- supplier/date PO grouping;
- PO draft creation;
- PO release.

Procurement may not silently rewrite:

- released Planning quantity;
- ingredient;
- controlled unit;
- service date;
- delivery destination;
- source lineage.

## 5. Allocation Family model

### 5.1 Family identity

The authoritative school-catering Procurement family grain is:

```text
service_date
+ delivery_location_id
+ ingredient_id
+ unit_id
```

For normal school catering, the delivery location corresponds operationally to the school, so this is the governed Atlas form of v1's date + school + ingredient family.

Different controlled units always remain separate families.

### 5.2 Family demand

A family total is derived exclusively from current released Purchase Handoff line revisions with the same family identity.

```text
family_demand = Σ current Purchase Handoff contributions
```

React must never reconstruct this aggregation from raw tables. The backend read model owns it.

### 5.3 Contribution lineage

Each family retains exact contribution references back to all Purchase Handoff line revisions that form the family total.

This creates the operator-friendly aggregate without destroying Planning traceability.

### 5.4 Supplier splits

An accepted family allocation contains one or more supplier splits:

- `supplier_id`;
- `allocated_quantity`;
- server-calculated `split_ratio`;
- decision origin;
- accepted source family version/fingerprint;
- eligibility evidence needed to prove the supplier was valid at acceptance time.

Required invariant:

```text
Σ supplier allocated_quantity = family_demand exactly
```

Every split is positive. A supplier may appear at most once in a family revision.

## 6. Default supplier recommendation

Atlas may recommend suppliers but may not silently authorize the decision.

For an untouched family, the default recommendation is:

```text
highest-priority currently eligible supplier = 100% of family demand
```

Priority is ranking evidence only. It is not a capacity percentage and must never be converted into automatic 60/40, 70/30 or other split ratios.

If there is no eligible supplier or priority is ambiguous, Atlas returns an exception and does not generate an authoritative allocation.

The recommendation becomes authoritative only when the operator:

- saves the family allocation manually; or
- runs the bulk confirmation action for untouched valid recommendations.

## 7. Rebalancing after upstream change

The family model exists partly to support safe deterministic rebalancing.

Example:

```text
old demand = 100 kg
Supplier A = 60 kg = 60%
Supplier B = 40 kg = 40%

new released demand = 120 kg
proposed rebalance:
Supplier A = 72 kg
Supplier B = 48 kg
```

The proposal is backend-derived and remains non-authoritative until operator confirmation.

### 7.1 Rounding rule

Use the governed quantity precision for the family/unit.

Calculate all but the final split according to the preserved ratios. The final split receives the exact residual so:

```text
Σ proposed splits = family_demand exactly
```

### 7.2 Eligibility change

If a previously accepted supplier is no longer eligible, Atlas must not silently redistribute that supplier's share. The family becomes `Cần phân bổ lại` and requires operator action.

### 7.3 Draft staleness

When an accepted family allocation changes, only PO drafts whose supplier/date contents depend on that changed allocation become stale.

Do not invalidate unrelated suppliers, dates, or the entire week.

Draft staleness should be derived from source revision currentness rather than maintained as an independently mutable workflow truth where possible.

## 8. Persistence design

### 8.1 Do not reuse the wholesale fulfilment-allocation aggregate

The existing `atlas_procurement.fulfilment_allocation_*` tables are tied to `dispatch_requirement_*` and the PA-05E one-supplier-per-requirement-line model.

School catering must not be forced through that shape.

### 8.2 Add a Procurement-owned family aggregate

The implementation should introduce the minimum school-catering allocation persistence required for:

- stable family identity;
- additive/revision-safe family state;
- Purchase Handoff contribution lineage;
- accepted supplier splits;
- split ratios;
- operator/command provenance;
- source version/fingerprint;
- current revision selection.

The exact table names may be finalized in the implementation plan, but the conceptual objects are:

- `PurchaseAllocationFamily`;
- `PurchaseAllocationFamilyRevision`;
- `PurchaseAllocationFamilyContribution`;
- `PurchaseAllocationSupplierSplit`.

Avoid a generic allocation engine, generic workflow engine, or polymorphic JSON authority.

### 8.3 Reuse the shared Purchase Order aggregate

Continue to use:

- `atlas_procurement.purchase_orders`;
- `purchase_order_revisions`;
- `purchase_order_lines`;
- `purchase_order_line_revisions`.

The shared PO concept is genuinely common across wholesale and school catering: it is the supplier-facing purchasing commitment.

School catering requires narrow extensions so a PO can represent:

```text
one supplier + one service date
→ multiple delivery locations/schools on PO lines
```

The wholesale behavior must remain compatible.

A PO line must preserve exactly one authoritative Procurement source path. Prefer explicit foreign keys and check/XOR constraints over an unconstrained generic `source_type/source_id` text pair.

## 9. PO lifecycle

### 9.1 Draft creation

`Tạo đơn mua` materializes PO **draft snapshots** from accepted balanced allocations in the selected date range/week.

The application works at week/range level, but the resulting aggregates are individual supplier/date POs.

Only accepted and balanced families participate.

Affected dates with unassigned, mismatched, stale, or invalid families are blocked from complete PO draft materialization and returned as exceptions.

PO draft quantities and supplier assignment are read-only derived decisions. The PO screen is not a second allocation editor.

If an allocation must change, the operator returns to `Phân bổ nhà cung ứng`.

### 9.2 Draft regeneration

If current allocations differ from a draft's source revisions, the draft is stale and cannot be released.

`Tạo lại đơn cần cập nhật` creates a successor draft revision only for affected supplier/date POs.

The implementation must not silently overwrite prior draft history.

### 9.3 Release

`Phát hành cho NCC` operates on exactly one PO aggregate transactionally.

On successful release the backend:

- revalidates current draft/source revisions;
- verifies supplier and source eligibility/currentness;
- generates the official PO number server-side;
- records the released snapshot;
- changes the PO to `RELEASED_TO_SUPPLIER`;
- emits domain/audit evidence;
- returns authoritative readback.

Bulk release is an application convenience that invokes independent per-PO release commands. Failure of Supplier B must not roll back already successful releases for Supplier A or Supplier C.

### 9.4 Supplier communication

For this slice, `RELEASED_TO_SUPPLIER` means Atlas has recorded the official supplier commitment.

Actual PDF export/download is the initial delivery mechanism and is not part of the transactional success condition.

No email/message delivery requirement is introduced yet.

### 9.5 Released-PO immutability

Once released, a PO cannot be regenerated from changed allocations by this slice.

Released-PO revision, cancellation, supplier replacement, partial rejection and acknowledgement are deferred to a later bounded Procurement contract.

## 10. Automated numbering policy

Operators do not type Atlas system identifiers or official document numbers.

UUID remains the internal identity. Human-readable codes are business references generated by the owning backend.

For Procurement:

- PO Draft: internal ID/reference only; official `document_number` remains null;
- PO release: official PO number generated server-side in the release transaction.

The release API must not accept an operator-entered official PO number.

The same principle should eventually apply to Ingredient, Supplier, School/delivery-location and other business codes. Existing Admin create APIs that require manually supplied `ingredient_code` or `supplier_code` are separate contract debt and must be corrected in a bounded Admin follow-up, not inside this Procurement slice.

Do not introduce one giant cross-domain numbering service unless later evidence requires it. Each owning domain should control its namespace while sharing only narrowly useful concurrency/format helpers.

Gapless numbering is not an MVP requirement unless a later legal/Finance contract explicitly requires it.

## 11. Public command design

The first connected school-catering slice requires five command responsibilities.

Exact final names may be reviewed during implementation planning, but their ownership and behavior are fixed by this design.

### 11.1 Planning: release school-catering Purchase Handoff

Recommended name:

`release_school_catering_purchase_handoff(request jsonb)`

Preconditions include:

- source Confirmed Need batch exists;
- source status is `RELEASED_FOR_PURCHASE_HANDOFF`;
- source approval/release evidence is current;
- source lines are current, positive and traceable;
- ingredient/unit/date/delivery-location facts are complete;
- no equivalent current handoff already exists.

Writes the exact Purchase Handoff snapshot and releases it to Procurement.

It creates no supplier allocation or PO.

### 11.2 Procurement: save one family allocation

Recommended name:

`save_school_catering_supplier_allocation(request jsonb)`

One family per command.

The payload submits the complete supplier split set for one expected current family version/fingerprint.

The server re-reads:

- Purchase Handoff contributions;
- family total;
- controlled unit;
- active supplier state;
- service-date eligibility/priorities;
- expected current family revision.

Validation requires positive unique supplier splits and exact balance.

The server calculates `split_ratio` values. The client does not author authoritative ratios.

### 11.3 Procurement: bulk-confirm untouched recommendations

Recommended name:

`confirm_school_catering_supplier_recommendations(request jsonb)`

This is a convenience command for selected date/school scope.

It confirms only untouched families whose recommendation is still unambiguous and current at execution time.

Stale, ambiguous, edited or ineligible families are skipped and returned as exceptions rather than overwritten.

### 11.4 Procurement: create PO drafts

Recommended name:

`create_school_catering_purchase_order_drafts(request jsonb)`

Scope: selected service-date range/week.

The server groups accepted current supplier splits into:

```text
supplier_id + service_date
```

and materializes/updates draft revisions only where required.

It creates no released supplier commitment.

### 11.5 Procurement: release one PO

Recommended name:

`release_school_catering_purchase_order(request jsonb)`

The request selects the current PO/draft revision and expected version; it does not accept `document_number`.

The backend generates the official number and release snapshot atomically.

## 12. Read models

Keep the connected Procurement read surface small and decision-first.

### 12.1 `get_school_catering_procurement_workbench`

Drives `Phân bổ nhà cung ứng` and returns shaped data including:

- date/week and school scope;
- family identity and total;
- ingredient/unit/delivery-location display facts;
- Purchase Handoff source currentness;
- contribution lineage summary;
- accepted supplier splits;
- prior split ratios;
- priority-1 recommendation;
- eligible supplier choices;
- allocation state;
- stale/rebalance proposal;
- blockers/warnings;
- backend-authorized allowed actions and disabled reasons.

The UI must not reconstruct family totals or supplier eligibility from raw records.

### 12.2 `get_school_catering_purchase_orders`

Drives `Đơn mua` and returns:

- supplier/date PO list;
- draft/released state;
- current revision/version;
- line count and school/destination detail;
- source allocation revision references;
- derived draft staleness;
- release eligibility;
- official number when released;
- export readiness;
- allowed actions and disabled reasons.

Audit/lineage detail remains available through progressive disclosure rather than dominating the main table.

## 13. UI design

Do not polish the dormant Procurement prototype into production unchanged.

Build the connected workspace around the two real operator jobs.

### 13.1 Stage 1: `Phân bổ nhà cung ứng`

Primary surface: dense Allocation Family table.

Recommended columns:

- Ngày giao;
- Trường / điểm giao;
- Nguyên liệu;
- Nhu cầu;
- Đã phân bổ;
- Còn lại / chênh lệch;
- NCC;
- Trạng thái.

Important filters:

- week/date range;
- multi-school scope;
- search;
- `Chưa phân bổ`;
- `Chưa đủ` / mismatch;
- `Đã đủ`;
- `Cần phân bổ lại`.

Selecting a family opens an attached split editor:

```text
Gạo · Trường A · 02/09
Nhu cầu: 100 kg

NCC A    60 kg    60%
NCC B    40 kg    40%

+ Thêm NCC
100 / 100 kg · Đủ
```

Primary actions:

- `Lưu phân bổ` for a manually edited family;
- `Xác nhận phân bổ đề xuất` for valid untouched recommendations;
- `Tạo đơn mua` when selected scope is ready.

Do not expose backend lifecycle ceremony such as separate Validate/Approve buttons when validation can be deterministic inside the commands.

### 13.2 Stage 2: `Đơn mua`

Primary surface: supplier/date PO list with selected PO detail.

Example:

```text
02/09
  NCC A · 18 dòng · Bản nháp
  NCC B · 12 dòng · Bản nháp
  NCC C · 21 dòng · Đã phát hành
```

Selected detail shows:

- supplier;
- service date;
- schools/destinations;
- ingredient lines;
- quantities/units;
- draft currentness;
- official number when released;
- blockers/warnings;
- export action.

Primary actions:

- `Tạo lại đơn cần cập nhật` for stale drafts;
- `Phát hành cho NCC` for a current releasable draft;
- optional multi-select / `Phát hành tất cả đơn sẵn sàng` as UI convenience over independent commands;
- PDF/export after release.

Do not create a separate `Xuất đơn cho nhà cung ứng` lifecycle tab. Export belongs to the PO.

### 13.3 Command outcomes and recovery

A toast alone is insufficient for important writes.

After family confirmation, draft generation or PO release, preserve a visible result state with:

- safe result message;
- affected object(s);
- resulting version/currentness;
- blockers/warnings;
- retry/reload guidance when applicable;
- next available action.

Required recovery classes include:

- capability/permission denied;
- source changed / stale version;
- allocation imbalance;
- supplier ineligible;
- no eligible supplier;
- ambiguous supplier priority;
- stale PO draft;
- PO already released;
- replay success;
- retryable transport/concurrency failure;
- unknown write outcome requiring authoritative reload.

## 14. Authorization, idempotency and transaction safety

Follow established Atlas command patterns rather than inventing a new framework.

Every command must preserve:

- authenticated subject resolution;
- active actor check;
- capability check;
- relational school/delivery-location scope or GLOBAL scope;
- exact payload allowlists;
- idempotency/replay behavior;
- `expected_version` optimistic concurrency where applicable;
- deterministic lock order;
- safe command receipts;
- domain events;
- audit events;
- no direct authenticated-role private-table writes;
- least-privilege Procurement/Planning runtime ownership;
- no SQL/RLS/runtime details in safe client responses.

Do not broaden PA-05E runtime privileges to make school catering convenient if a narrowly scoped school-catering command/runtime boundary is safer.

## 15. Validation invariants

The implementation must fail closed on at least:

- missing/unreleased Purchase Handoff source;
- cross-wired Purchase Handoff contribution lineage;
- duplicate family identity under the same current source scope;
- negative/zero family demand where a purchasable family is expected;
- family unit mismatch;
- duplicate supplier inside one accepted family revision;
- non-positive supplier split;
- supplier inactive;
- supplier not eligible on service date;
- allocation sum below family demand;
- allocation sum above family demand;
- stale family/source version;
- draft generated from incomplete/stale allocation;
- PO line source that does not belong to the selected supplier/date PO;
- stale PO draft release;
- manual document number supplied where prohibited;
- duplicate official PO number;
- released PO silently overwritten.

## 16. Testing strategy

Testing must prioritize the real cross-stage operation, not only isolated local units.

### 16.1 Backend contract tests

Prove:

- Purchase Handoff materializes exact Confirmed Need release facts;
- family aggregation is deterministic and preserves contribution lineage;
- 100% priority-1 recommendation is advisory until confirmation;
- family split save is atomic;
- split sum must exactly equal family total;
- duplicate/ineligible suppliers fail closed;
- split ratios are server-calculated;
- rebalancing preserves ratios when suppliers remain eligible;
- final residual closes rounding exactly;
- ineligible prior supplier forces manual reallocation;
- PO draft grouping is supplier + service date with multi-school lines;
- stale allocation invalidates only affected drafts;
- PO release generates official number server-side;
- released PO snapshots cannot be silently regenerated;
- PA-05E wholesale tests remain green.

### 16.2 Connected frontend tests

Prove:

- routed Procurement screen uses connected API, not fixtures;
- family table is the primary work surface;
- parent date/school filters are display/read scope only and do not mutate backend truth;
- recommendation is not persisted until an explicit operator action;
- edited split keeps complete family payload;
- exact imbalance prevents save/draft generation;
- stale/rebalance messaging is clear;
- PO draft cannot be released when stale;
- bulk release reports mixed success without pretending atomic all-or-nothing behavior;
- no official PO-number input is rendered;
- command outcomes and recovery states persist visibly;
- Vietnamese operator vocabulary is used instead of raw backend status names.

### 16.3 Cross-stage rehearsal

At minimum rehearse:

```text
Menu / Attendance / Pantry
→ Need Generation
→ Confirmed Need
→ Chuyển sang lên đơn
→ Purchase Handoff
→ Allocation Family
→ default recommendation
→ manual supplier split
→ PO draft
→ PO release
```

Then change an upstream Planning quantity and verify:

- affected family gets new current demand;
- prior ratios are proposed correctly;
- operator confirmation is required;
- only affected supplier/date drafts become stale;
- unreleased drafts can be regenerated;
- already released POs remain historical commitments and are not rewritten.

## 17. Explicit non-goals for the first connected slice

Do not add:

- supplier acknowledgement workflow;
- partial/rejected supplier confirmation;
- released-PO amendment/cancellation/replacement;
- supplier capacity optimization;
- automatic proportional splits based on priority;
- automatic commitment without operator confirmation;
- price/tax/payment terms/invoice/Finance;
- Warehouse receiving;
- Dispatch documents or trips;
- a generic orchestration engine;
- a generic allocation framework;
- a generic cross-domain numbering service;
- Retool changes;
- Live OPS changes;
- unrelated Admin code-number migrations.

## 18. Implementation decomposition recommendation

The final implementation plan should keep one architectural workstream but split execution into reviewable slices to reduce Codex cost and regression risk:

1. **School-catering Purchase Handoff backend boundary** — command/readback and Planning integration.
2. **Allocation Family persistence + reads** — family aggregation, contributions, recommendation, staleness.
3. **Supplier allocation commands** — manual family save + bulk default confirmation + rebalance behavior.
4. **PO draft persistence extension** — shared PO aggregate compatibility, supplier/date grouping, multi-school line sources.
5. **PO release + automated numbering** — per-PO release, official number, immutable snapshot.
6. **Connected Procurement UI** — two-stage workbench using the shaped reads/commands.
7. **Cross-stage and hosted browser certification** — realistic Planning→Procurement rehearsal and responsive UI review.

Do not implement all seven as independent conceptual redesigns. They are slices of this single approved contract and should share one exact contract vocabulary.

## 20. Acceptance definition for this design

The first connected school-catering Procurement capability is complete when an authorized operator can:

1. release confirmed school-catering demand through a durable Purchase Handoff;
2. see backend-generated Allocation Families at the correct date/location/ingredient/unit grain;
3. accept the priority-1 default or split one family across multiple eligible suppliers;
4. preserve/reuse split ratios for safe rebalancing after upstream demand changes;
5. bring all relevant families to exact balanced allocation;
6. create PO drafts for a selected week/date range;
7. inspect supplier/date multi-school PO drafts;
8. release each current PO independently;
9. receive a backend-generated official PO number;
10. export the released PO;
11. retain exact traceability from PO line → supplier split/family → Purchase Handoff → Confirmed Need;
12. prove that upstream edits propagate to unreleased Procurement work without silently rewriting released supplier commitments.
